# Business Plan Template

## Intent
Use Walter Grant's How to Write a Winning Business Plan framework to produce a complete, investor-ready business plan that articulates your market opportunity, competitive positioning, operational strategy, and financial projections.

## Inputs
- Business concept and value proposition
- Market research data (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- Competitor landscape and differentiation points
- Founding team bios and organizational structure
- Historical financials (if operating) or industry benchmarks
- Funding amount sought and intended use of proceeds

## Process (SOP)
1. Step 1: Write the executive summary last but place it first — one to two pages capturing the problem, solution, market size, business model, traction, team, and funding ask.
2. Step 2: Define the problem and opportunity — quantify the pain your target customer experiences and the cost of the status quo.
3. Step 3: Describe your solution and product — focus on the customer benefit, not technical specifications; include development stage and roadmap.
4. Step 4: Conduct market analysis — estimate TAM, SAM, and SOM with bottom-up reasoning (number of target customers × revenue per customer), not top-down percentages alone.
5. Step 5: Analyze competitors — map direct, indirect, and substitute competitors; identify your unfair advantage and barriers to entry; avoid claiming you have no competitors.
6. Step 6: Define your business model — revenue streams, pricing strategy, unit economics (CAC, LTV, gross margin), and path to profitability.
7. Step 7: Outline the go-to-market strategy — primary channels, sales motion, customer acquisition cost assumptions, and key partnerships.
8. Step 8: Detail the management team — highlight relevant domain expertise, prior exits or results, and any critical hires needed with the funding.
9. Step 9: Build financial projections — 3-year P&L forecast with monthly granularity for year 1; include revenue drivers, cost structure, headcount plan, and cash flow statement.
10. Step 10: Write the funding ask — specify the round size, use of proceeds (broken into categories with percentages), key milestones the capital unlocks, and expected runway.

## Outputs
- A complete 15-25 page business plan document
- Executive summary suitable for standalone distribution
- Three-year financial projections with assumptions documented
- Competitive analysis matrix
- Funding ask with use-of-proceeds breakdown

## Evidence
Source: "How to Write a Winning Business Plan" by Walter Grant. Key concepts: executive summary structure, TAM/SAM/SOM analysis, competitive positioning and barriers to entry, bottom-up market sizing, unit economics (CAC/LTV), financial projection methodology, funding ask and use of proceeds.

## Examples
A health-tech startup writes a plan projecting $12M ARR by year 3. Their TAM analysis uses bottom-up reasoning: 40,000 target clinics × $3,000 average contract value = $120M SAM. They seek $2.5M Series A: 45% engineering, 30% sales, 15% operations, 10% G&A — unlocking 18 months of runway to reach $5M ARR.

## Metrics
- Completeness of all 10 plan sections
- Realism of financial projections vs. industry benchmarks
- Clarity and specificity of the funding ask
- Bottom-up market sizing defensibility
- Document length and readability (investor can digest in under 10 minutes)

## Anti-Patterns
- Writing the executive summary first — it will be rewritten anyway; write it last when you know the full story
- Claiming a $50B TAM with no bottom-up validation
- Listing competitors but claiming you have none — this signals naivety to investors
- Overly optimistic hockey-stick projections with no underlying driver model
- Hiding the funding ask or being vague about use of proceeds
- A plan that reads like a product brochure instead of an investment thesis
