# Scaling Up Framework

## Intent
Use Verne Harnish's Scaling Up framework (adapted from Mastering the Rockefeller Habits) to diagnose and resolve the four core challenges of scaling: People, Strategy, Execution, and Cash.

## Inputs
- Current company size and growth trajectory
- Organizational structure and leadership team composition
- Financial statements (P&L, cash flow, balance sheet)
- Existing strategic plans and meeting cadences
- Employee and customer satisfaction data

## Process (SOP)
1. Step 1: Diagnose which of the four scaling disciplines (People, Strategy, Execution, Cash) is your primary bottleneck — address them in that priority order.
2. Step 2 (People): Ensure you have the right people in the right seats — use a scorecard for every role tied to the company's core values and strategic priorities.
3. Step 3 (People): Establish a leadership team that meets weekly with a structured agenda — priorities, metrics, and accountability, not status updates.
4. Step 4 (Strategy): Define your company's core values, purpose, and brand promise — these must be observable, measurable, and reinforced in every decision.
5. Step 5 (Strategy): Build a One-Page Strategic Plan capturing your 3-5 year vision, 1-year plan, quarterly rocks, and the single biggest priority.
6. Step 6 (Execution): Set quarterly "rocks" (3-5 key priorities) for the company and each individual — no more, or focus fragments.
7. Step 7 (Execution): Establish a meeting rhythm — daily huddles (5-10 min), weekly tactical sessions, monthly strategic reviews, and quarterly offsites.
8. Step 8 (Execution): Create an accountability scorecard for every rock with a named owner and a red/yellow/green status.
9. Step 9 (Cash): Identify your "cash accelerator" — the single lever that speeds up cash collection (e.g., payment terms, deposits, inventory turns).
10. Step 10: Monitor cash weekly and forecast 13 weeks ahead at minimum; growth without cash flow management kills companies.

## Outputs
- A One-Page Strategic Plan aligned across the leadership team
- A quarterly rocks framework with named owners and status tracking
- A sustainable meeting cadence that scales accountability
- A 13-week rolling cash forecast

## Evidence
Source: "Scaling Up" by Verne Harnish. Key concepts: People/Strategy/Execution/Cash framework, One-Page Strategic Plan, quarterly rocks, Rockefeller Habits (meeting rhythm, red/yellow/green scorecards), cash accelerator, core values and purpose.

## Examples
A 40-person agency hits a growth plateau. They diagnose Execution as the bottleneck. They implement daily 8-minute stand-ups, weekly tactical meetings, and quarterly rocks. Within two quarters, project delivery slips drop by 60% and the company exceeds its revenue target for the first time.

## Metrics
- Percentage of rocks completed on time per quarter
- Employee engagement survey scores (people dimension)
- Cash conversion cycle length
- Revenue per employee trend
- Leadership meeting adherence to agenda/timebox

## Anti-Patterns
- Hiring for skills before ensuring cultural/values fit
- Letting the One-Page Plan gather dust — it must be reviewed and updated quarterly
- Holding meetings without clear owners, metrics, or accountability
- Trying to execute more than 5 rocks per quarter — this guarantees none get done
- Managing cash annually instead of weekly — cash crises emerge with no warning
- Copying another company's strategy without adapting to your market position
