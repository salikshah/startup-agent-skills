# Growth Hacking Loop

## Intent
Use Sean Ellis's growth hacking methodology to systematically find and scale your highest-leverage growth channel through rapid, structured experimentation across the AARRR funnel.

## Inputs
- Product or service with at least minimal traction
- Access to analytics and user behavior data
- Cross-functional team (engineering, marketing, product)
- Defined north-star metric
- List of potential growth hypotheses

## Process (SOP)
1. Step 1: Form a growth team with a dedicated growth lead, engineer, marketer, and product manager — this team owns growth across functions.
2. Step 2: Identify your north-star metric (e.g., weekly active users, monthly recurring revenue) that best captures delivered value.
3. Step 3: Map the full AARRR funnel (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral) and identify the weakest, highest-impact stage.
4. Step 4: Brainstorm a large quantity of growth ideas (aim for 20+) targeting the weakest funnel stage.
5. Step 5: Prioritize ideas using an impact-confidence-ease scoring framework (ICE or similar) — select the top 3-5 for testing.
6. Step 6: Design each experiment with a clear hypothesis, success metric, and minimum detectable effect.
7. Step 7: Run experiments rapidly (1-2 week cycles) — ship MVPs of growth features, not final products.
8. Step 8: Analyze results rigorously; kill what does not move the metric, double down on what does.
9. Step 9: Scale winning experiments by increasing investment incrementally and monitoring diminishing returns.
10. Step 10: Systematize wins into repeatable playbooks and move to the next funnel stage.

## Outputs
- A prioritized pipeline of growth experiments with ICE scores
- A tested, documented set of winning growth levers
- Scaled user acquisition and/or retention
- A repeatable growth playbook

## Evidence
Source: "Hacking Growth" by Sean Ellis & Morgan Brown. Key concepts: growth team structure, high-tempo testing, ICE prioritization, AARRR pirate funnel, north-star metric, diminishing returns curves.

## Examples
A B2B SaaS company discovers activation is their bottleneck — signups are strong but few users complete onboarding. They run 12 activation experiments in 6 weeks. A guided checklist + progress bar lifts activation by 22%. They scale it globally and document it in their growth playbook.

## Metrics
- Number of experiments run per month (target: 10+)
- Win rate of experiments (healthy range: 10-30%)
- North-star metric growth rate week-over-week
- Cost per acquisition trend across scaled channels

## Anti-Patterns
- Running experiments without a clear hypothesis
- Declaring victory after one positive data point without statistical confidence
- Letting HiPPOs (highest paid person's opinions) override experiment results
- Neglecting retention to chase acquisition — growth without retention leaks
- Running too many experiments simultaneously, making results uninterpretable
- Copying growth tactics from other companies without testing them in your context
