# Traction Channel Prioritization

## Intent
Use Gabriel Weinberg's Traction bullseye framework to systematically identify, prioritize, and test the most promising of 19 traction channels until you find one that drives significant growth.

## Inputs
- Product or service with a clear value proposition
- Target customer profile (demographics, behaviors, where they congregate)
- Budget and timeline for traction testing
- Existing analytics and customer acquisition data
- Team capabilities (content, PR, sales, engineering)

## Process (SOP)
1. Step 1: Brainstorm all 19 traction channels — viral marketing, PR, unconventional PR, SEM, SEO, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing, targeting blogs, business development, direct sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building, social and display ads, and offline ads.
2. Step 2: For each channel, estimate three variables based on research: potential reach (how many target customers it touches), cost to test, and expected conversion rate.
3. Step 3: Place each channel into one of three bullseye rings: inner ring (top 3-5 most promising), middle ring (worth testing later), outer ring (not viable for your situation).
4. Step 4: Select one inner-ring channel as your primary focus — do not test multiple channels simultaneously; you need focus to get signal.
5. Step 5: Design a low-cost, fast experiment for the primary channel — define the hypothesis, the minimum success metric, and the experiment budget.
6. Step 6: Run the experiment within 2-4 weeks — generate at least 100 data points (visitors, leads, or customers) to evaluate the channel.
7. Step 7: Analyze results against three criteria: customer acquisition cost (CAC), scalability (can it grow 10x?), and sustainability (does it compound or plateau?).
8. Step 8: If the channel fails the criteria, return to the outer ring, select the next most promising channel, and repeat the experiment.
9. Step 9: When a channel passes all three criteria, pull the bullseye — invest aggressively and scale it until it saturates before diversifying.
10. Step 10: Document every experiment result to build an institutional knowledge base of what works and does not work for your specific product-market combination.

## Outputs
- A completed 19-channel bullseye worksheet with ring assignments
- Experiment log with results for each channel tested
- A validated primary traction channel with CAC and scale data
- A prioritized testing queue for future channels

## Evidence
Source: "Traction" by Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares. Key concepts: 19 traction channels, bullseye framework (three rings), channel prioritization criteria (CAC, scalability, sustainability), rapid low-cost testing, engineering as marketing, pull the bullseye principle.

## Examples
A B2B analytics startup tests three channels: content marketing (CAC $45, scalable, compounding), LinkedIn ads (CAC $180, limited scale), trade shows (CAC $500+, not repeatable). They pull the bullseye on content marketing, publish 4 articles/month for 6 months, and grow to 2,000 signups/month with CAC trending downward.

## Metrics
- Number of channels tested within budget and timeline
- CAC per validated channel
- Channel scalability assessment (can it grow 10x current volume?)
- Time from first experiment to validated primary channel
- Customer volume generated by the primary channel month-over-month

## Anti-Patterns
- Testing 3+ channels simultaneously — you will not get clean signal on any of them
- Falling in love with a channel because it is trendy when your customers are elsewhere
- Ignoring channels that seem unsexy (email, blogs, targeting blogs) — often the highest-ROI channels are the least glamorous
- Pulling the bullseye before reaching statistical significance — 10 data points is not a pattern
- Abandoning a channel after one failed experiment — test variations before declaring a channel dead
- Choosing channels from competitor behavior instead of customer research
