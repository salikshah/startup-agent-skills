# Doing Content Right SOP

## Intent
Use Steph Smith's Doing Content Right methodology to build a content operation that prioritizes distribution, repurposing, and sustainable output over content volume.

## Inputs
- Brand positioning and target audience definition
- Core expertise or unique perspective of the founder/team
- Available content creation capacity (time, skills, budget)
- Existing content assets (if any)
- Chosen primary distribution channel

## Process (SOP)
1. Step 1: Define your content pillars — 3-5 core themes that intersect your expertise with your audience's persistent interests, not your product's features.
2. Step 2: Adopt distribution-first thinking — before creating any piece, decide where and how it will be distributed; content without distribution reaches no one.
3. Step 3: Choose one primary distribution channel to master first — owned (newsletter, blog), earned (guest posts, PR), or paid (ads) — instead of spreading thin.
4. Step 4: Create a batch content calendar — block time for ideation, creation, and distribution as separate activities, not one marathon.
5. Step 5: Produce long-form "cornerstone" content pieces (guides, frameworks, deep dives) that can be atomized into smaller assets.
6. Step 6: Repurpose systematically — one long-form piece becomes 5-10 social posts, newsletter editions, video scripts, and community discussions.
7. Step 7: Build audience capture mechanisms — convert social followers to newsletter subscribers, newsletter readers to community members.
8. Step 8: Establish a consistent publishing cadence you can sustain for 12+ months, not 3 months of heroic effort followed by silence.
9. Step 9: Engage in the comments and communities where your audience already gathers — distribution includes participation as well as broadcasting.
10. Step 10: Review analytics monthly to identify which pillars and formats drive actual audience growth, then double down.

Edge cases: if a pillar produces no audience growth after 8 weeks, retire it. If capacity drops, cut cadence before quality. If a channel changes its algorithm, return to the owned channel and rebuild reach there.

## Outputs
- A documented set of 3-5 content pillars
- A sustainable content calendar with batch production schedule
- A repurposing playbook (1 piece -> 10+ assets)
- A growing owned audience (newsletter, community)
- Monthly content performance review

**Pre-flight checklist:**
- [ ] 3-5 pillars defined from audience interests
- [ ] One primary channel chosen
- [ ] 12-month cadence documented
- [ ] Repurposing workflow documented
- [ ] Audience capture path live

## Evidence
Source: "Doing Content Right" by Steph Smith. Key concepts: content pillars, distribution-first thinking, repurposing flywheel, owned vs. earned vs. paid channels, batch production, sustainable cadence, audience capture.

## Examples
A solo consultant defines 3 pillars: pricing psychology, B2B sales messaging, and founder productivity. She publishes one 2,000-word guide per week, repurposes each into 8 LinkedIn posts and a newsletter edition. In 6 months her newsletter grows to 5,000 subscribers and generates 3 inbound leads per month.

## Metrics
- Primary channel growth rate (newsletter subscribers, followers) month-over-month. Target: 10%+ for the first 6 months.
- Repurposing ratio (assets produced per cornerstone piece). Target: 8+.
- Engagement rate on primary channel. Target: 3%+.
- Inbound leads or conversations attributed to content. Target: 3+ per month by month 6.
- Content production consistency (weeks published / weeks planned). Target: 90%+.

## Anti-Patterns
- Choosing content topics from product features instead of audience interests
- Posting on 5 platforms with no depth on any — master one first
- Creating content without a distribution plan
- Publishing in bursts then going silent for months
- Treating repurposing as lazy — it is the core efficiency engine
- Writing for algorithms instead of humans
- Ignoring audience replies and community engagement
